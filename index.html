🔍 Sprint Review

0:00
Use the timer to track and record how long your review takes.

Before you start, align on:

  • What are we going to showcase?
  • Who is going to present or demonstrate the new features?

Flow:

  1. Opening & Context: The Product Owner explains what backlog items are "Done" and which are not.
  2. The Demonstration / Hands-On Session:
    • Developers show the working software or product increment.
    • Better yet, make it hands-on — let stakeholders test or interact with the increment themselves.
    • Avoid heavy slides or scripted corporate presentations; focus strictly on the working product.
  3. Discussion & Feedback:
    • Talk about progress toward the broader Product Goal.
    • Discuss market changes, new constraints, or user feedback.
  4. Backlog Adaptation:
    • The Product Owner leads a discussion on what to pull into the next sprint based on what was just learned.

      What the Product Owner brings

      The Product Owner should bring the ticket to the table with only the business intent and user boundaries. The Product Owner should not try to guess the technical constraints.

      Example:

      • User Story: “Social Login”
      • Context & Value: Why we are doing this based on user feedback.
      • Acceptance Criteria: Scenarios 1 and 2 define the functional boundaries and how the work will be evaluated or tested.
      • Out of Scope: Crucial for the Product Owner to define to prevent scope creep.

      What Developers create during Planning

      Sub-tasks are strictly owned by the Developers. The Product Owner should almost never write or dictate sub-tasks.

      • Sub-tasks are the technical roadmap for the Sprint.
      • Keep sub-tasks concise and actionable instead of writing long descriptions.

      Example:

      • Sub-task 1: Set up Google OAuth API credentials.
      • Sub-task 2: Create frontend login buttons.
      • Sub-task 3: Update the database schema for social IDs.
